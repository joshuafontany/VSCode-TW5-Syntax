# The share of corpus tokens every ruling in every ledger stands on between them. The count may
# fall and may never rise.
#
# `still.js` opens with a warning: the stopping condition can be met by ruling generously. The guard
# written against that scored a key's SHAPE — wildcards and literal segments — and ranked ground
# exactly backwards: `meta.codeblock.*` carries a wildcard and stands on 3.5% of tokens, while
# `meta.paragraph.tiddlywiki5` carries none and stood on 18%. Worse, it examined only a key that
# VANISHED, pairing it with whatever replaced it, so an ADDITION passed unweighed however much
# ground it claimed — and an addition is the shape a generous ruling actually takes. A ruling on
# 18% of the corpus went in that way, off one cut in one file.
#
# THIS COUNTS TOKENS, NEVER SCOPES. A token wears several scopes at once, and a union summed per
# scope counted one token once per scope it carried — reading 100.9% of a corpus, which is the only
# reason the fault surfaced rather than settling in as a number nobody questioned.
#
# THE NUMBER MEASURES A PROXY AND WANTS READING AS ONE. Ground share rises with how COMMON a
# construct stands as well as with how loosely a ruling reads: `meta.variable.call.*` names one
# construct precisely and stands on 12% of tokens, because this corpus runs sigil-dense. So the
# count guards a direction rather than a level — a ruling that needs more ground than the tree
# already holds answers for it in writing, here.
#
# Seated at 55.8% on 2026-09-08, the day the ledgers moved to the kind vocabulary. It read 79.4%
# under container keys, and keying by CAUSE dissolved the difference rather than any ruling changing
